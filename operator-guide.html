Start here — train a new operator in 30 minutes
Read this section and you can run the system. Everything below it is reference. LIMEN Helix is a discovery instrument: it scans clinical-neuroscience pathologies and treatments, maps them onto other fields (business, environment, population, supply-chain, medicine…), and surfaces the transfers that nobody has tried yet as candidate research, patents, grants, and investment ideas. It is not an oracle — it does not claim its findings are proven. Your job is to read what it surfaces, decide what is worth pursuing, and print the documents that matter.
Your job in one sentence. Review what the system surfaces, judge what stage each thing is at, pursue the ones worth pursuing — and never let the screen overstate what is actually known. The system is built to be honest about its own confidence; your job is to keep it that way.
The four states you will see everywhere. Every claim, mapping, and candidate transfer carries one of four labels. We keep everything — an unproven idea is a reason for research, not a reason to hide it. The label tells you the next move, not whether to trust the screen blindly:

• PROVEN — evidence supports it. Strengthen it, advance it, cite it.
• UNPROVEN — a claim exists but isn't confirmed yet. This is a research target: design the test.
• UNKNOWN — not examined yet, a gap. Go look.
• IMPOSSIBLE — contradicted or refuted. Still useful: study why it fails — that's where the analogy breaks. Kept, never deleted.

On screen these appear as verdict badges: VERIFIED→proven · THEORETICAL→unproven · PENDING/UNVERIFIABLE→unknown · DISPUTED/FABRICATED→impossible. Hover any badge to see what it means.
Your first session, step by step
1
System health is produced by a local audit script (scripts/audit-system-vitals.mjs) that scores 12 organs — there is no live Vitals page in this build (/vitals returns 404). Treat it as a maintainer tool, not a daily surface. Your day-to-day start is the Master Brain Inbox (next step).
2
Open PATTERN PROPOSALS. This is your review worklist — the bridge patterns the system proposes for research / investment. Read the top few items.
3
Click into one item to open its COMPANY PORTAL. Read the bridge pattern (why the system thinks this brain↔business mapping applies) and the engine outputs (patent / grant / investment / research tabs).
4
Check the state badge before you believe a claim. A confident-looking paragraph with a PENDING/UNKNOWN badge is a lead, not a fact. A PROVEN badge means it's backed by evidence.
5
Decide: PRINT (download a filing-ready draft), REFRESH (ask for more depth on the next cycle), or DECLINE (stop surfacing it). See § 3 for exactly what each button does.
6
For pure research leads, open TREATMENT DISCOVERY. The four buckets at the top show the whole corpus separated by state. Sort nodes by residual count, open one, read the chain, and print the node if a transfer looks worth testing.
Worked example. You open the inbox and the top item is a candidate patent memo for a company, badged UNPROVEN. You open its portal, read the bridge ("amygdala hyper-reactivity ↔ this firm's over-exposure to volatility") and the patent tab. It reads well but it's a candidate, not a granted claim — the badge says so. You decide it's worth protecting, click Draft Candidate Patent Memo, and save the DOCX to your LIMEN Helix Files folder. You did not file anything with the USPTO — you drafted a memo. That distinction is the whole discipline.
Golden rules — never break these.
1. A draft is a draft. Buttons that say "Draft", "Mark", "Pursue", or "Save" only write locally. Nothing here files, submits, sends, or buys.
2. PENDING / UNKNOWN ≠ proven. Don't act on an unconfirmed claim as if it were confirmed.
3. Never delete a finding for being unproven. Unproven and impossible are research, not garbage.
4. If a label looks wrong (e.g. a confident claim that should be hedged), that is a bug worth reporting — the screen's authority must match the evidence.
If something looks wrong: note the surface + what you saw, and report it. (System health is a local audit script, scripts/audit-system-vitals.mjs — not a live page in this build.) You cannot break the system by clicking PRINT/REFRESH/DECLINE — those are safe. Nothing on these pages reaches an external party.

The big picture
LIMEN Helix is a cross-domain discovery instrument running as an autonomic body. It maps clinical-neuroscience pathologies and treatments onto other domains, surfaces transfers that have no analog yet as candidate research, mints patent / grant / investment / research seed artifacts, gates them by phase + readiness + salience, surfaces a prioritized inbox, and prints filing-ready documents on operator approval. The job is not to prove every output before you see it — it is to surface what may matter and label honestly what stage each thing is at. The autonomic chain (sense → bridge → mint → gate → respond → heal) runs on demand / locally in this build — see § 4 for the real scheduling status.
Keep everything — separate by epistemic state. LIMEN never deletes a finding for being unproven; unproven is a reason for research, not to hide. Four states (full detail in Start here): PROVEN → strengthen & cite · UNPROVEN → design the test · UNKNOWN → go look · IMPOSSIBLE → study why it fails. Verification writes the state; it never deletes the finding.
The closed loop:
sense → bridge (brain ↔ business) → mint (patent / grant / investment / research) → gate (phase + readiness + salience) → respond (your PRINT/REFRESH/DECLINE) → identify missing → autonomously author new patterns → heal → remember

Scheduling note: this chain is not wired to an automatic cron in this build (no .github/workflows in canonical — see § 4); the scripts run on demand. Operator role: review the inbox, approve/reject Claude-authored pattern proposals, print the documents that matter, refresh the ones that need more depth.

Every button explained
On artifact cards (inbox + portal page)
PRINT
Downloads a DOCX to your browser. Calls Claude Sonnet via /api/print-from-pattern to expand the seed artifact into a full document for the research (OSF preregistration) or investment (thesis memo) lane. Browser asks where to save — point at your LIMEN Helix Files desktop folder. Takes 30-90 seconds because it's a real long-form Anthropic call.
REFRESH
Regenerate this specific artifact with greater detail. Adds to the action queue; the next run of the refresh step calls Claude with portal context (suppliers, customers, kernel phase) + bridge mechanism to rewrite the seed. After it runs, the inbox/portal-page render the improved version. (Run on demand — no automatic cron in this build.)
DECLINE
"Don't surface this again." Marks the action complete with declined: true. Future inbox sorts deprioritize declined items.
On pattern proposals (Claude-authored new bridges)
APPROVE · MERGE INTO LIBRARY
The proposal pattern becomes part of bridge-patterns.json. The next bridge-build pass (run on demand) re-matches it against all 767 portals; new artifacts mint anywhere it matches. Once approved, it's permanent unless you manually remove it.
REJECT
Marks status: REJECTED. Library untouched. The proposal stays visible in the Recently Rejected section.
↗ SOURCE PORTAL
Open the company portal that triggered the proposal — see why the body wanted a new pattern for this entity.

5
Documents the system can print
Click PRINT on any artifact card. The browser downloads a DOCX you can open in Word — save to your LIMEN Helix Files desktop folder.
PATENT · USPTO Pro Se / Micro Entity utility specification
18-25 pages. Full sections: cross-reference, field of invention, background, brief summary, brief description of drawings, detailed description (60-70% of total), claims (18-20 of them: independent method claim 1, dependents 2-15, apparatus claim 16, system claim 17, computer-readable-medium claim 18), abstract. Calibrated for independent inventor with no operating revenue — qualifies for USPTO Micro Entity 75% fee reduction (37 CFR 1.29).
GRANT · NIH or NSF SBIR Phase I application
20-30 pages. Project Summary · Project Narrative · Specific Aims (1 page, canonical opening sentences) · Research Strategy (Significance + Innovation + Approach, the last being 6-8 pages of the document) · Commercialization Plan summary · Bibliography (no fabrication) · Budget Justification · Equipment & Facilities. Phase I is proof-of-feasibility. Maximum $314,363, 6 months. Targets pre-revenue sole-founder reality.
LOAN · SBA Microloan or SBA Express application
15-25 pages. Executive Summary · Borrower Profile · Use of Proceeds (Sources-and-Uses table that balances) · Sources of Repayment (honest: founder's external income, not non-existent business cash flow) · Business Plan Summary (5-7 pages with conservative bootstrap projections) · Personal Financial Statement template · Credit Not Available Elsewhere narrative (SBA SOP 50 10 8 compliant) · Collateral · Risk Factors + Mitigants · Lender Attachments checklist. Up to $50K Microloan or up to $500K SBA Express. Avoids 7(a) which requires operating history.
RESEARCH · OSF / AsPredicted preregistration
8-15 pages. Title · Hypotheses (falsifiable, directional) · Rationale · Data Collection · Studies · Analysis Plan (frequentist or Bayesian, decision criteria) · Sample size justification with power analysis · Confounds + mitigation · Exclusions · Open Materials/Data commitment · Timeline. Calibrated for independent researcher without institutional affiliation — uses public data, no IRB if applicable, single-author.
Cost reality: Each PRINT generates 6,000-10,000 words via Anthropic Sonnet. Typical cost: $0.50-2.00 per document. The first 5-10 are essentially free (existing Anthropic credits). Beyond that, see § 8 on budget controls.
6
Bridge pattern proposals · how the library grows
The library starts at 15 patterns. Claude expands it.
1
Every 6h, author-bridge-patterns.mjs --max=3 runs. It looks at portals with kernel signal but no existing pattern match (the K3 frontier — currently 306 portals).
2
For up to 3 such portals per tick, it asks Claude Opus to propose a new bridge pattern with the same JSON schema as existing patterns. Strict rules: no fabricated citations, must be distinct from existing patterns, must include testable indicators.
3
Proposals land at /pattern-proposals.html as PENDING_REVIEW. Operator clicks APPROVE to merge into bridge-patterns.json or REJECT to retire.
4
On approve: pattern enters the library. Next bridge-build pass re-matches it across all 767 portals. If it fires, the operator sees new bridges + new artifacts in the inbox at next tick.
Cross-domain: The pattern schema is domain-agnostic. targetDomain can be business, environment, population, supply-chain, life-expectancy, public-health, ecology. Same engine; different target. Adding patterns for environmental indicators or population health metrics is just adding entries — no engine change required.
7
Autonomous portal regeneration
When the body realizes it needs portals it doesn't have.
Every 6h tick, autonomous-portal-regen.mjs finds:
  • CB rows pointing at slugs with no portal file (~188 currently)
  • Portals with kernel signal present but no bridge match AND thin functional network (signal exists but the body can't reason about it)
It writes a queue to _autonomous-regen-queue.json. The build-fractal-portals runner can drain that queue when invoked with --queue <path> — but it's NOT auto-invoked because each portal costs Anthropic API. You decide when to spend.
8
AI budget controls
The body uses Anthropic (primary), OpenAI (verification), and Grok (retrieval). Each cron tick has a hard budget.
Per-tick limits (1-hour rotating window, configurable via Vercel env vars):
  • LIMEN_AI_TOKENS_PER_TICK · default 500,000 tokens per hour
  • LIMEN_AI_CALLS_PER_TICK · default 50 calls per hour
Default spend ceiling at Anthropic Sonnet rates:
  • 500K tokens × ($3/M input + $15/M output blended) ≈ $3-7 per cron tick worst case
  • 4 ticks per day → $12-28 per day worst case
  • Real usage is much lower — most ticks use < 50K tokens
Tune the env vars in Vercel project settings if needed. When budget hits the cap, the orchestrator refuses further calls until the next hour. The body never bricks itself.
