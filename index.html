01 / WHAT IS LIMEN HELIX?

More input is not
more intelligence.

Organizations can acquire without clearing, expand without maintenance, and accelerate without restraint. Helix studies whether the brain’s regulatory architecture can help identify these failures in business and civilization systems.

“Structurally lesioned” means a missing or disconnected control mechanism in the model.

The research tests structural homology: whether the same control relationships can be identified across neural, business, and civilization systems. Each mapping needs a mechanism, its regulatory partner, and evidence of what happens when that partner is missing.

01 / ATTENTION

What deserves a signal?

A system needs a way to filter information before it reaches decisions. Too little filtering floods attention. Too much filtering hides the change that matters.

02 / INHIBITION

What stops the runaway?

Growth and action need counterweights. Helix examines whether the mechanisms that restrain activity are present, missing, or disconnected.

03 / RECOVERY

What gets cleared?

Learning requires more than adding. It also requires retiring failed responses, releasing obsolete commitments, and checking outcomes before repeating a decision.

02 / MAKE THE MECHANISM CONCRETE

An energy shock.
A test of attention.

Consider a grid operator facing a flood of alerts. The important question is not simply how much data arrives. It is which signal reaches a decision, which noise is suppressed, and what evidence confirms that the filter worked.
