The Business Operations Atlas (in your artifact sidebar) maps all 20 LIMEN Helix civilization domains — Energy, Finance, Economy, Trade, Agriculture, Infrastructure, Industry, Technology, Science, Communication, Education, Medicine, Population, Environment, Governance, Defense, Intelligence, Law, Culture, Religion — at three altitudes, the way an anatomy reference zooms from whole body to individual structures:

  • 100,000 ft — each domain's role in civilization, its interdependencies with the other 19 domains, and its regulation model.
  • 10,000 ft — the specific business types inside the domain, each with its NAICS 2022 code, working definition, business model and day-to-day operations, external regulation and self-regulation, and archetypal firms — plus the domain's full regulator table.
  • Ground level — the positions, color-coded across five tiers (Governance, Executive, Management, Professional, Operational), each with its 2018 SOC code, reporting line, actual duties, and typical credentials.

Its Foundations sections cover the anatomy shared by every company (12 legal forms, the universal 9-tier authority ladder, the 10 core functions, size classes) and the three-layer theory of how business regulates itself: state regulation from above, self-regulation from within, and mutual market regulation from the side.

The six canonical source systems

These are the most comprehensive classifications of business and work in existence — the taxonomies governments and markets themselves use to see the economy. Every NAICS and SOC code in the Atlas is a pointer into them.

NAICS 2022 — North American Industry Classification System 1,012 industries · 20 sectors

The definitive taxonomy of what businesses DO. Every establishment in North America is classified by its primary production process. Maintained by the U.S. Census Bureau with Canada and Mexico; revised every 5 years.
census.gov/naics

ISIC Rev. 4 / Rev. 5 — International Standard Industrial Classification 21 sections · global

The United Nations' worldwide equivalent — the root taxonomy from which national systems (NACE in Europe, ANZSIC, etc.) descend. The common language for comparing economies across countries.
unstats.un.org

SOC 2018 — Standard Occupational Classification 867 occupations · 23 major groups

The definitive taxonomy of what WORKERS do — every job in the U.S. economy maps to a SOC code. Maintained by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
bls.gov/soc

O*NET-SOC — Occupational Information Network ~900+ occupations × 200+ descriptors

The deepest occupational database known — for every occupation it catalogs tasks, tools, knowledge, skills, abilities, work context, education, and wages. Sponsored by the U.S. Department of Labor.
onetonline.org

GICS — Global Industry Classification Standard 11 sectors · 163 sub-industries

How the FINANCIAL world classifies companies — MSCI and S&P's standard used to sort every publicly traded firm on Earth for indexing and investment.
msci.com/gics

APQC Process Classification Framework 13 categories · 1,000+ processes

The standard taxonomy of business OPERATIONS — every activity inside a firm (develop strategy, market & sell, manage supply chain, manage HR…) in a numbered hierarchy used for benchmarking worldwide.
apqc.org/pcf

Where the reusable data lives

The full structured dataset behind the Atlas is saved in your Business professor and scientist project, so any future Claude session can reuse it without regenerating a word:

  • claude/business-operations-atlas-data.json — the complete dataset: universal frameworks, legal forms, the authority ladder, core functions, regulation layers, plus all 20 domains with every business type, regulator, and position.
  • claude/business-operations-atlas-README.md — the guide describing the structure and how to extend it.

Ask any session in the project to, for example, "turn the Finance domain into a Word doc," "add a 21st domain," or "build a slide deck from the Atlas data" — it can build directly from these files.
