These levers are the human gate. Every operator mandate in lib/operator-fleet.js ends "I never spend, send, or act on my own", and Kai's ends "I open a human gate; I never walk through it." The fleet cannot throw anything on this page. You can.

The board

Six pieces of state a human can flip. Each reads its position from the server — a lever is disabled and says reading… until the real state arrives, and never sits ON for decoration. A throw is reconciled against the server's answer; a rejected write springs the lever back and says why.

Below the line: wired and in use, but with no on/off of their own — they are exercised by the levers above, so each names the one that governs it. Then the three with no code path at all, which are builds rather than settings.

The desks

Every venture this deployment runs, grouped by what it is FOR rather than by handler name — 175 routes and 19 scheduled jobs. Each desk's state is the WORST state among its jobs, read off the same ledger as the levers: observed, never inferred from a schedule. A desk with no schedule says on demand, which is an answer, not a fault.

Elsewhere

Real properties this board cannot reach. Separate deployments with their own credentials — nothing here can read or change them. Listed anyway, because "not on the board" must never read as "does not exist".

The floor

The twenty named operators, from /api/fleet. Each carries a posture bounded to abstain, watching, has a call, or wants you — and that last one is an operator asking for the gate above. Those sort to the front.
